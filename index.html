Who I Am

I'm a Mathematician and computational scientist turned AI researcher, trained in advanced mathematics and applied to the frontier of machine learning. My research asks: when AI models fail, how do we know — and how do we fix it?

My Master's in Mathematics at Andhra University focused on Cryptography, Diophantine Equation and Number Theory. It is empasized by building a cryptosystem based on diophantine equation and group theory for secure system in cryptography.

My Master's thesis at Skoltech (2025-2026) focused on detecting and localizing hallucinations in Large Language Models using probing classifiers over hidden states. Alongside this, I published on adversarial attacks against learned image compression (NeuroInformatics 2025) and on data-driven sparse modeling for two-phase fluid flows (accepted at IEEE ADACIS 2025).

I'm actively seeking PhD positions in AI, Mathematics, Physics, and Computer Science, as well as Engineering roles as an AI Engineer, Data Scientist, Research Engineer.

Research & Publications

Published at NeuroInformatics 2025 2025

Robustness of AI Image Compression: Non-Linear Perturbation

A. Rarivonjy et al.

We study the vulnerability of learned image compression codecs (Cheng2020, HiFiC) to adversarial perturbations via FGSM and PGD attacks. Surprisingly small L∞-bounded perturbations collapse PSNR from ~35 dB to ~13 dB, revealing a critical gap between perceptual quality and compression robustness. We propose multi-loss attack objectives and show transferability across codecs.

Accepted at IEEE ADACIS 2025 2025

Data-Driven and Delayed Sparse-Regularized Models for Two-Phase Flow Property Prediction

A. Rarivonjy et al.

We apply sparse system identification (SINDy) with time-delay embedding and L1-regularization to predict properties of two-phase flows (gas-liquid pipelines). Our method recovers governing equations from noisy sensor data without prior knowledge of the physics, outperforming black-box neural networks in out-of-distribution scenarios.

Dynamical SystemsSINDyScientific MLFluid Mechanics
Under Review · DAMDID 2026 2026

Regularized and Basis Function Representations for Time-Varying Coefficient Recovery in Dynamical System ODEs

A. Rarivonjy et al.

We introduce a regularized basis-function framework for recovering time-varying parameters in ODE systems from noisy observations. The method combines Fourier/wavelet basis expansions with Tikhonov regularization to handle measurement noise and parameter non-stationarity, with applications in epidemiology and neuroscience.

ODEsInverse ProblemsRegularizationScientific Computing
Master's Thesis 2025

Hallucination Detection and Localization in Large Language Models

A. Rarivonjy — Skoltech

A probing-based framework for detecting and localizing factual hallucinations in LLMs at inference time without external knowledge retrieval. We analyze internal hidden states of Qwen2.5, extract geometric features (curvature, anisotropy, layer-wise variance), and train lightweight probes achieving robust classification. We further propose token-level localization to pinpoint where in a generation hallucination begins.
